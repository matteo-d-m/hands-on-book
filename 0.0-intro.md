This website contains an open book on TMS-EEG data preprocessing. It is a reader-friendly version of teaching materials for the Brain Stimulation & Multimodal Electrophysiological Recording course, [Master's degree in Cognitive Science](https://corsi.unitn.it/en/cognitive-science), University of Trento, where the author served as a teaching assistant in the 2025 and 2026 editions.

The aim of this book is to provide a tutorial introduction to the main steps of a basic TMS-EEG preprocessing pipeline, explaining their conceptual background alongside their Python implementation. 

> [!NOTE]
> This book is an introductory educational resource. Therefore, it has no pretense of being exhaustive or of reaching maximum precision, and the language is intentionally informal. The target audience are absolute beginners (for example, undergraduate students) who need to understand what TMS-EEG data look like, what challenges they pose, and how to start tackling them. 
>
> Moreover, this book has no pretense of endorsing one particular pipeline as the most effective, elegant, or scientifically validated. The operations listed in this book are merely the most common, which cannot be ignored by an introductory presentation.

The data used throughout the book come from one condition (`C1_M1`) and one subject (`S02`) of [Zazio et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2021.06.025). Such data are publicly available through [G-Node Gin](https://gin.g-node.org/) at [this](https://gin.g-node.org/AgneseZazio/ZazioMiniussiBortoletto2021) link and freely reusable under a [Creative Commons CC0 1.0 Universal license](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/deed.en). 

The preprocessing pipeline implemented in this book is loosely inspired by the one described in Zazio et al. (2021) under section "_2.3.1. TMS-EEG preprocessing_". The original pipeline has been largely preserved for consistency, but also adapted to the constraints of a short hands-on educational activity like the one that inspired this book. The most relevant deviation from the original pipeline is the omission of the [SOUND](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.10.021) and [SSP-SIR](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.05.028) algorithms, which could not be treated with clarity in the time at hand. However, this might change in the future.
