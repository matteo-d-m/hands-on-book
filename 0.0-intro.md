This website contains a tutorial introduction to the preprocessing of TMS-EEG data. It is a reader-friendly version of the materials for the hands-on activity offered in the Brain Stimulation & Multimodal Electrophysiological Recording course, [Master's degree in Cognitive Science](https://corsi.unitn.it/en/cognitive-science), University of Trento. The aim of this activity is to introduce the main steps of a basic TMS-EEG preprocessing pipeline, explaining their conceptual background alongside their Python implementation. 

> [!NOTE]
> This website has no pretense of endorsing one particular pipeline as the most effective, elegant, or scientifically validated. The aim is 100% educational and the target audience are absolute beginners. Therefore, the preprocessing steps included in the pipeline are merely the most common, which cannot be ignored by an introductory presentation.

The data used throughout the website come from one condition (`C1_M1`) and one subject (`S02`) of [Zazio et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2021.06.025). Such data are publicly available through [G-Node Gin](https://gin.g-node.org/) at [this](https://gin.g-node.org/AgneseZazio/ZazioMiniussiBortoletto2021) link and freely reusable under a [Creative Commons CC0 1.0 Universal license](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/deed.en). 

The preprocessing pipeline implemented in this website is loosely inspired by the one described in Zazio et al. (2021) under section "_2.3.1. TMS-EEG preprocessing_". The original pipeline has been largely preserved for consistency, but also adapted to the constraints of a short hands-on educational activity like the one that inspired this website. The most relevant deviation from the original pipeline is the omission of the [SOUND](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.10.021) and [SSP-SIR](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.05.028) algorithms, which could not be treated with clarity in the time at hand. However, this might change in the future.
